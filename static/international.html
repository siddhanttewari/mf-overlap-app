1. The big idea

🧒

Like you are 5: Imagine your town has one famous ice-cream street. If you only ever buy from that street and it has a bad summer, you are stuck. The world has lots of ice-cream streets. Owning a little of many streets means one bad summer does not ruin everything.

That is international investing: owning companies from other countries, not only India. Three reasons people do it:

🌱 More streets

Markets do not all rise and fall together. In the first half of 2026 the Nifty 50 fell 6.56% while South Korea's KOSPI rose 86.82% and Taiwan's index rose 58.63%.

🏭 Different shops

Some businesses barely exist in India: chip makers, global software, big pharma. Abroad you can own them.

💵 Another currency

Your foreign holding is priced in dollars, euros or yen. When the rupee moves against them, your rupee value moves too, up or down.

This is education, not a recommendation. Foreign markets can also fall hard, and rules and costs add friction. Past returns above are history, not a promise.

🧒

3. Pick a basket

A fund is a basket of many companies. Here are the common baskets, in plain words.

🇺🇸 The US basket: S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100

🧒

Like you are 5: The S&P 500 is a basket of about 500 giant American companies. The Nasdaq-100 is a smaller basket, mostly technology, with the biggest tech firms given the biggest slices.

  • S&P 500: the five biggest companies (NVIDIA, Apple, Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet class A) are about 27.2% of it.
  • Nasdaq-100: the same five are about 45.2%, so it leans harder on a few names.
  • The catch: both are heavily tech and both depend on the same handful of companies.

Weights from SlickCharts, viewed 16 Oct 2026, a third-party aggregator; check against the fund's factsheet.

🌍 The world basket: a world index

🧒

Like you are 5: One big basket with a little bit of everything: America, Japan, Britain, Canada and more. You do not pick countries; the scoreboard picks for you by company size.

  • MSCI World (developed countries) is 72.94% US. MSCI ACWI (adds emerging markets) is 64.21% US.
  • Why people like it: one holding, thousands of companies, automatic updates as countries grow or shrink.
  • The catch: "world" does not mean "equal". You mostly own America plus a sprinkle of others.

Net returns to 30 Sep 2026 (in US dollars): ACWI 16.75% in one year and 12.38% a year over ten; World 15.24% and 12.81%. Past performance does not predict the future.

🌏 The emerging markets basket

🧒

Like you are 5: Emerging markets are countries that are still growing up: building factories, roads and phones. The basket holds their biggest companies. It is a different playground from America.

  • Top 10 (MSCI EM, 30 Sep 2026): TSMC 15.63%, Samsung Electronics 7.55%, SK Hynix 5.90%, Tencent 2.74%, Alibaba 1.86%, MediaTek 1.82%, Samsung preferred 0.94%, Delta Electronics 0.94%, China Construction Bank 0.86%, Hon Hai 0.79%. Together 39.03%.
  • By country: Taiwan 28.94%, South Korea 21.40%, China 19.80%, India 10.65%, Brazil 4.09%.
  • India fact: in the May 2026 index data, for the first time in 26 years, no Indian stock was in the top 10 (Business Today, 9 Jun 2026).
  • The catch: concentrated in chips, and some of its companies are ones a US fund also owns (TSMC is in both).

Net returns to 30 Sep 2026 in US dollars: 29.22% in one year but 9.09% a year over ten, versus 12.81% for MSCI World. Emerging markets can lead for a year and lag for a decade.

🇪🇺 🇯🇵 Single-region baskets: Europe, Japan, China tech, Taiwan

🧒

Like you are 5: Instead of the whole pizza, you order just one slice: Europe, Japan, or one country.

  • Europe and Japan: MSCI EAFE's biggest names are ASML 3.28%, HSBC 1.59%, Roche 1.37%, Shell 1.28% and Novartis 1.22%. It is much less top-heavy than the US: its ten biggest add to only 14.20%.
  • China tech: the Hang Seng TECH basket has 30 companies; its biggest holdings include NetEase, Tencent, Xiaomi, Meituan and BYD at roughly 8% each (31 Aug 2026).
  • Catch: a one-country basket is a bigger bet than a world basket. Few of these Indian-listed funds take fresh money today.

🎨 Themed and ready-made baskets

🧒

Like you are 5: Some baskets are picked around a story (global innovation, consumer brands, water) or put together for you by an app, like a pre-packed lunchbox.

  • Themed funds: a manager chooses companies for a theme. They hold fewer, bigger bets than an index.
  • Curated baskets from platforms such as Vested and INDmoney: ready-made groups of foreign stocks and ETFs. They are still foreign stocks and ETFs, so LRS, TCS and the tax rules below still apply.
  • Questions to ask any ready-made basket: who picks the stocks, what is in it today, what does it cost in total, and how do I leave?

4. The 4 doors into world markets

The same basket can be reached through different doors. Each door has its own rules, cost and queue. Status below is as last verified; it changes week to week.

1

🏦 Indian international mutual funds and fund of funds

Mostly shut

🧒 The government gave all Indian fund houses one shared piggy bank for foreign investing. It is full. So the doors are mostly locked.

Indian mutual funds can put only about USD 7 billion in total into overseas securities. The limit was reached in 2022 and has stayed full. As funds redeem, a little room opens and a fund may reopen briefly.

How shut? In early July 2026 Value Research counted 12 of 66 funds open. On 4 September it said only 1 of the 60 overseas equity funds it tracks was open. On 25 September it listed 4 open or reopening for lump sums and new SIPs (direct plans, as of 24 Sep): Baroda BNP Paribas Aqua FoF (global water, open from 1 Sep, no cap stated), and three Invesco India FoFs reopening on 28 Sep with a Rs 10 lakh per PAN per day limit: Global Equity Income, Pan European Equity and Global Consumer Trends. None was US-focused.

Tax (check your own case): international fund of funds are taxed as long-term gains after 24 months at 12.5% per Kotak Mutual Fund.

2

📈 International ETFs listed on NSE and BSE

Open, but priced wild

🧒 These are baskets you buy on the Indian stock market like a share. But the supply of baskets is fixed, so when many people want one, the price can float far above what is inside.

Indian international ETFs have a separate USD 1 billion limit, and Value Research reports it filled in April 2024. Since then, market makers cannot create new units, so prices can drift above the true value (NAV). See the next section for the September 2026 numbers.

3

✈️ Direct investing abroad through LRS

Open, with paperwork

🧒 You send your own money out of India, within a yearly allowance, and buy foreign shares or ETFs yourself, usually through an app.

Under the RBI Liberalised Remittance Scheme you can send up to USD 250,000 a year per person. Above Rs 10 lakh in a financial year, 20% TCS applies to investment remittances (a deposit you can claim in your tax return). US dividends face 25% withholding with a Form 67 credit, foreign shares held more than 24 months are taxed at 12.5% on gains, you must report them in Schedule FA, and US-listed holdings can carry US estate tax exposure above USD 60,000.

This door is not subject to the Indian mutual fund cap. Apps such as Vested and INDmoney offer this route.

4

🏝️ GIFT City funds, AIFs and PMS

Open, big tickets

🧒 GIFT City is a special zone in Gujarat where India lets funds work with foreign rules. It has its own doorway to world markets.

Funds in GIFT City are regulated by IFSCA, not SEBI, so they sit outside the USD 7 billion mutual fund cap. Residents still use LRS to invest. Minimums are high: for example USD 150,000 for a restricted scheme (USD 40,000 for employees of the manager) and USD 75,000 for PMS.

5. The ETF premium trap (September 2026)

🧒

Like you are 5: Imagine a lunchbox that has Rs 100 of food inside but there are only 10 lunchboxes in the whole school, and 1,000 kids want one. Kids start paying Rs 300 for a Rs 100 lunchbox. That extra Rs 200 is the premium. When the kids lose interest, the price falls back to Rs 100, and whoever paid Rs 300 loses.

Value Research reported that the Motilal Oswal Nasdaq Q50 ETF's premium went from about 19.5% on 4 September to 235.5% on 18 September, while the stocks inside barely moved. After touching Rs 471.99 on 21 September, its price fell the full 20% limit three days in a row, from Rs 396.30 on 18 September to Rs 202.92. A rule change on 7 September, which set the daily price band around the previous day's price instead of the NAV, made the premium compound. SEBI plans to move the band back to the previous day's NAV from 1 April 2027.

Three habits that protect you

  • Compare price with NAV before you buy. A 40% premium means 40% of what you pay is not the stocks inside.
  • Remember fund of funds are exposed too. A fund that holds these ETFs values them at market price, so its returns can jump with the premium. Value Research cited a Mirae S&P 500 Top 50 FoF up 25.6% in a week while its ETF's NAV moved 0.4%.
  • Open does not mean good. The Value Research story ends with the same caution: an open fund may not stay open, and being open is not a reason to buy.

6. The overlap trap: same toppings, three pizzas

🧒

Like you are 5: You order a Nasdaq pizza, an S&P pizza and a world pizza, thinking you got three different dinners. But every pizza has the same big toppings: NVIDIA, Apple, Microsoft, Amazon. You paid for three, but you are eating one.

Where else does overlap sneak in?

  • Stacking a US fund on a world fund: the world fund is already about 64% to 73% US.
  • TSMC everywhere: it is the number 1 holding of emerging markets (15.63%), number 6 in the whole-world index (1.87%) and in active global funds too.
  • Emerging markets and India: India is 10.65% of the emerging markets index, so it overlaps your Indian funds.
  • Platform baskets: a curated basket of US tech stocks plus a Nasdaq ETF is, again, the same companies.

7. Read inside the box, not the label

🧒

Like you are 5: A box that says "Chocolates" might have toffee inside. Always open the box and look.

When we rebuilt our international data from the funds' own disclosures this month, several labels did not match what is inside:

  • "US Bluechip" is not an index clone. ICICI Prudential US Bluechip Equity Fund's biggest holding at 30 Sep 2026 was Microsoft at 2.97%, followed by Danaher, Estee Lauder, LPL Financial and Broadcom. NVIDIA, the S&P 500's biggest weight at 7.44%, is not in its top 20.
  • A Taiwan fund without TSMC in its top 10. Nippon India Taiwan Equity Fund's top holding on 31 Jul 2026 was MediaTek at 7.34%, then MPI, Chroma ATE, Nanya Technology and Accton. TSMC was not in the top 10.
  • "Global" funds are heavy on US. ABSL International Equity Fund's top holdings at 30 Sep 2026 were NVIDIA 4.79%, Alphabet 4.31% and Microsoft 2.93%.
  • A REIT fund can hold Asian REITs, not American ones. Kotak International REIT FoF held 99.35% in Smam Asia REIT Sub Trust at 30 Sep 2026.
  • Many "global" funds are a basket holding one other basket. Kotak Global Innovation FoF held about 77% in Wellington Global Innovation Fund and 22% in Geninnov Global Master Fund. So check the cost at both layers.

Holdings from the AMCs' monthly disclosures via Sharpely, PGIM India, Nippon India and Trackinsight; see sources. Always check the latest factsheet.

9. Big words, small meanings

Index

A scoreboard list of companies, like the S&P 500 or MSCI World. You cannot buy it directly. Funds copy it.

ETF

A basket of shares that you buy on the stock market like a single share.

Mutual fund

A basket run by a fund house. You buy it from the fund house or an app at the day’s NAV.

Fund of funds (FoF)

A basket that buys other baskets. Two layers, so check both layers of cost.

NAV

The true price tag of a fund: the value of everything inside, per unit.

Premium / discount

Paying more (premium) or less (discount) than the NAV when you buy an ETF on the market.

Developed vs emerging markets

Developed: rich, mature countries (US, Japan, UK). Emerging: faster-growing, riskier countries (Taiwan, Korea, China, India, Brazil).

LRS

Liberalised Remittance Scheme: your yearly allowance of USD 250,000 to send money abroad.

TCS

Tax Collected at Source: a deposit the bank collects when you send money abroad. You get credit for it in your tax return.

Overlap

How much of two funds is the same companies. Less overlap means more real variety.

Currency risk

Your foreign holding is priced in another currency. When the rupee moves, so does your rupee value.

GIFT City

India’s special financial zone in Gujarat (IFSC) where funds follow IFSCA rules and deal in dollars.

10. Questions people ask

Can Indians invest in international markets?
Yes, through four main doors: Indian mutual funds and fund of funds that invest abroad, international ETFs listed in India, direct investing through the RBI Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS, up to USD 250,000 a year per person), and GIFT City funds. Several of these doors are partly or fully shut at any given time, so check what is open before you plan.
Why are most Indian international mutual funds closed to new money?
Indian mutual funds are allowed to put only about USD 7 billion in total into overseas securities, and that limit has been full since 2022. Once a fund house has used its share, it has to stop or ration new money. Value Research counted 12 of 66 funds open in early July 2026, and listed only 4 as open or reopening on 25 September 2026. None of those four was US-focused.
What is an international ETF premium and why does it matter?
An ETF has a real price tag called NAV and a market price you pay on the exchange. When the ETF cannot create new units, buyers can bid the market price above the NAV. In September 2026 the Motilal Oswal Nasdaq Q50 ETF traded as much as 235.5% above its NAV before falling to 68.3%. Buying at a big premium means you pay for scarcity, and the premium can shrink fast.
What is the difference between a world index, an emerging markets index and the S&P 500?
The S&P 500 holds about 500 large US companies. A world index such as MSCI World holds about 1,249 companies from developed countries and MSCI ACWI holds about 2,414 from developed and emerging countries. An emerging markets index such as MSCI Emerging Markets holds about 1,165 companies from countries like Taiwan, South Korea, China and India. Even in a world index, the US makes up roughly 64% to 73% of the weight.
Is a world index fund enough, or do I need US, Europe and Asia funds separately?
A world index already contains the US, Europe, Japan and more, so adding a separate US fund on top mostly doubles up on the same big US companies. That doubling-up is the overlap problem this site measures. Which mix is right depends on your goals, time horizon and tax position, so treat this as education and speak to a registered adviser.
Does international investing have extra tax and paperwork?
Yes. For direct investing through LRS, remittances above Rs 10 lakh in a financial year attract 20% TCS (a prepayment you can claim in your tax return). US dividends face withholding tax, foreign shares held for more than 24 months are taxed as long-term gains at 12.5%, and you must report foreign assets in Schedule FA. US-listed holdings can also create US estate tax exposure for non-US persons above USD 60,000. See our GIFT City vs direct US investing guide for details.
