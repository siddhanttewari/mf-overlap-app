Large Cap Fund Overlap: Do All Large Cap Funds Hold the Same Stocks?

Short answer: largely, yes. We analysed 14 Large Cap mutual funds on OverlapIQ and found that most pairs overlap 60-80%. Here's the data.

Why Large Caps Overlap the Most

SEBI defines Large Cap funds as those investing at least 80% in the top 100 companies by market capitalisation. This constraint means every Large Cap fund manager is picking from the same pool of ~100 stocks. The top 20 stocks by market cap — HDFC Bank, Reliance, ICICI Bank, TCS, Infosys, Bharti Airtel, ITC, L&T, SBI, Bajaj Finance — collectively represent 60-65% of the Nifty 100. No Large Cap fund can avoid them.

What Differentiates Large Cap Funds?

If 50-60% of every Large Cap fund is the same, what makes up the remaining 40%? This is where fund manager conviction comes in:

  • Axis Bluechip has higher allocation to consumer names like Avenue Supermarts, Titan, and Asian Paints — a quality-growth tilt
  • HDFC Top 100 leans towards PSU and cyclical names like NTPC and Power Grid — a value tilt
  • Mirae Asset Large Cap has a balanced approach with slightly higher allocation to auto and industrial stocks
  • Quant Large Cap takes contrarian positions with higher energy and commodity allocation

These differences account for 20-40% of the portfolio and are the only source of differentiated returns. When you hold two Large Cap funds, you're paying for two sets of these small conviction bets while the other 60% is identical.

The Verdict: One Large Cap Fund is Enough

Unless you have a specific reason (like transitioning between funds or wanting both active and passive exposure), one Large Cap fund is sufficient. If you want broad large-cap exposure at the lowest cost, a Nifty 50 Index Fund captures 90%+ of what any active Large Cap fund holds, at a fraction of the expense ratio (0.1-0.2% vs 0.8-1.5%).

Pair your single Large Cap or Index fund with a Mid Cap or Small Cap fund for genuinely different stock exposure. The overlap between a Large Cap and Small Cap fund is typically just 5-15% — now that's real diversification.
