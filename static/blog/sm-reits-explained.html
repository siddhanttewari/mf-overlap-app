SM REITs Explained: Small and Medium REITs, the ₹10 Lakh Ticket and the Risks

SM REITs let you own a share of a single leased office building for ₹10 lakh. Here is how SEBI's framework works, what has been issued, how payouts are taxed and why concentration is the main risk.

📊 Direct Answer

An SM REIT is a SEBI-regulated, exchange-listed scheme that owns one or two completed, leased properties worth ₹50 crore to under ₹500 crore, with a ₹10 lakh minimum investment. Listed REITs hold many assets and can be bought a unit at a time. Three PropShare schemes have come to market (₹353 crore, ₹473 crore and ₹244.65 crore). The main risks are single-asset concentration, tenant exits, thin trading and returns that include your own capital.

The Short Answer

An SM REIT (Small and Medium REIT) is a listed real estate trust framework that SEBI introduced in March 2024 so investors can own a share in a single completed, leased property worth ₹50 crore to under ₹500 crore, with a ₹10 lakh minimum. A regular listed REIT owns a portfolio of many large assets and you can start with the price of one unit. The SM REIT trades diversification and an easy entry ticket for a concentrated, higher-yield-on-paper bet on one building.

Three schemes from one manager, Property Share, have come to market so far, and the track record is short. This guide explains the rules, shows what has been issued, and sets out the risks, including one live example from a scheme's own filing. For listed REITs, see our six-REIT comparison.

How an SM REIT Works

One licensed manager can run several schemes, and each scheme owns one property through a special purpose vehicle (SPV). You buy units of the scheme you like, not of the manager, so you pick a building, its tenants and its lease terms. Units are listed on the stock exchange, which gives you an exit route, but a unit costs about ₹10 lakh, so the market is thin and a listing is not the same as liquidity.

The SPV must pass at least 95% of its distributable cash to the scheme, and the scheme pays out all of it, declared at least quarterly. At least 95% of assets must be completed, revenue-generating property, so you cannot buy into an under-construction project. Borrowing, net of cash, is capped at 49% of scheme assets, and above 25% needs a credit rating and a simple majority of unitholders. Each scheme needs at least 200 unitholders outside the manager's circle. Capital is typically returned when the property is sold, often after four to six years (Business Standard).

A Live Example of What to Read in the Filings

In October 2026, PropShare Titania declared ₹24,046.03 per unit for the quarter ended 30 September, a total of about ₹10.73 crore, which its filing says consists of interest income and debt repayment. As our own arithmetic, four such quarters would be about ₹96,200 a year, or roughly 9.1% of the top of the IPO price band of ₹10.6 lakh, in line with the 9% the scheme projected. The caveat is in the filing: the payout includes debt repayment, meaning part of it is capital coming back, so it should not be read as a pure income yield.

The same filing flagged a legal risk

The filing also disclosed a provisional demand of about ₹46.27 crore from MSEDCL, the Maharashtra electricity distributor, over alleged unauthorised electricity use at G Corp Tech Park from 2016 to 2026. The trust is contesting it in the Bombay High Court and has not made a provision, because management expects a favourable outcome. For a scheme that raised ₹473 crore, a demand of this size is material, and it shows how a single-asset scheme concentrates one building's problems on you.

We cannot say how the case will end, and nothing here means the scheme is a poor investment. The point is that a one-asset vehicle has no portfolio to absorb a claim like this, so read the contingent-liability notes, not just the yield.

Payouts and Tax

Business Standard's illustration of a typical payout is about 20% interest and 80% return of capital, and the Titania filing similarly describes interest plus debt repayment. Interest is taxed at your slab rate with TDS. Return of capital is not taxed when received but reduces your cost of acquisition, which raises your capital gain when you sell. Listed units sold after 12 months attract long-term capital gains tax of 12.5%, and sales within a year attract 20%. Keep each quarter's distribution statement, because the split between income and capital decides what you owe; our REIT taxation guide explains the mechanics.

The Risks, Honestly

  • One building. A vacancy, a legal claim or a damaged asset hits the whole scheme. A listed REIT spreads that risk across many assets.
  • Re-leasing. Tenants usually sign lock-ins of around three years. If a tenant leaves when it ends, the manager must find a replacement, perhaps at a lower rent.
  • Liquidity. A ₹10 lakh unit trades thinly. Celestia opened its first trading day at a 4.77% discount to its issue price, which shows exit prices can differ from offer prices.
  • Valuation and exit. The return of your capital depends on selling the property in four to six years at a good price.
  • Short track record. The framework dates from March 2024, so there is little history on how SM REITs behave through a full cycle.
  • Return of capital. A high payout can include your own money. Judge the scheme on the total return, not the yield.

A financial adviser quoted by Business Standard suggested limiting SM REIT exposure to about 5% of net worth (excluding the home you live in), with a horizon of at least 10 years, and that most retail investors should stay with listed REITs until the new structure builds a track record. That is one adviser's view, not a rule.

How to Read an SM REIT Offer Document

  • Who are the tenants, when do their leases and lock-ins expire, and what is the weighted average lease expiry?
  • How does the contract rent compare with market rent? Above-market rent raises the chance a tenant leaves or renegotiates.
  • What are the valuation and the price you are paying against it?
  • How much debt is there, at what rate, and when does it mature?
  • What are the contingent liabilities, litigation and statutory demands?
  • What is the manager's own stake and who are the related parties?
  • What is the planned exit, and what happens if the property cannot be sold on time?
  • Is the projected yield based on rent alone, and how much of the payout is return of capital?

Who It Might Suit

An SM REIT is a niche instrument. It may suit an investor who already holds diversified financial assets, has ₹10 lakh to commit without needing it back soon, understands the property and tenant, and can tolerate one building's risk. It rarely makes sense as a first real estate holding. Compare it with direct property and real estate funds, and size any position using our allocation guide. Frequently Asked Questions

What is an SM REIT?

An SM REIT is a Small and Medium Real Estate Investment Trust under a SEBI framework introduced in March 2024. Each scheme owns one or two completed, leased properties worth at least ₹50 crore and less than ₹500 crore, units are listed on the exchange and the minimum investment is ₹10 lakh.

What is the minimum investment in an SM REIT?

₹10 lakh. In practice one unit of a Property Share scheme has cost about ₹10 lakh to ₹10.6 lakh. A listed REIT, by contrast, can be bought one unit at a time, at a price of a few hundred rupees.

Is an SM REIT safer than a regular REIT?

No. It is generally more concentrated, because a scheme holds one or two properties instead of a portfolio, and units are thinly traded. Its protections come from SEBI's rules on completed assets, a borrowing cap of 49% and distribution requirements. Returns are not guaranteed and capital can be lost.

Are SM REIT returns taxed like REIT returns?

Broadly yes. Interest is taxed at your slab rate, return of capital reduces your cost of acquisition, and listed units sold after 12 months pay 12.5% long-term capital gains tax, with a 20% rate for shorter holdings. The mix of components in each payout differs, so check each distribution statement and confirm with a chartered accountant.

People Also Ask

Can I sell SM REIT units any time?
They are listed, so you can place a sell order when the market is open, but with units priced near ₹10 lakh and few buyers, you may not get your price or a quick fill. Check the trading volume before you rely on the exit.
How many SM REITs are there in India?
Four managers had SEBI licences by January 2025: Property Share, REPL, Emberstone and Strata. The schemes we could confirm as listed are three from Property Share: Platina, Titania and Celestia.
Why did PropShare Celestia list below its issue price?
It opened at ₹9,99,900 on 24 April 2026, 4.77% below the ₹10.5 lakh issue price, after an issue subscribed 1.33 times. Thin trading in a ₹10 lakh unit and a modest subscription are possible reasons, but the sources do not say why.
