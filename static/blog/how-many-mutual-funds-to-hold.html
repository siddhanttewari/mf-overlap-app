How Many Mutual Funds Should You Hold? The Overlap Problem

The average Indian mutual fund investor holds 6-8 schemes. Our data shows that beyond 4-5 well-chosen funds, every additional scheme increases overlap more than it adds diversification.

The "More Funds = More Safety" Myth

It feels intuitive: if one fund is good, five must be better, and ten must be the safest. This logic works for asset classes (equity + debt + gold genuinely diversifies), but it breaks down within the same asset class. Adding a fourth Large Cap fund to your portfolio doesn't make you safer — it just multiplies your exposure to HDFC Bank and Reliance Industries.

Here's what actually happens as you add more funds:

Notice how unique stock count barely increases after 5 funds — from ~150 to ~170 — while average overlap shoots up from 50% to 70%. You're paying 3 more expense ratios for just 20 additional stocks.

The Sweet Spot: 3-5 Funds

For most Indian investors, 3 to 5 well-chosen funds provide optimal diversification with minimal overlap. The key is choosing funds from different categories, not different AMCs.

Portfolio Template: 3 Funds (Conservative)

  • 1 Index Fund (Nifty 50 or Nifty Next 50) — broad market beta, lowest cost
  • 1 Mid Cap Fund — growth potential, different stock universe
  • 1 ELSS Fund — tax savings + equity exposure

Expected pairwise overlap: 10-25%. Excellent diversification.

Portfolio Template: 5 Funds (Aggressive)

  • 1 Flexi Cap Fund — core allocation, all market caps
  • 1 Mid Cap Fund — mid-cap alpha
  • 1 Small Cap Fund — high growth, high risk
  • 1 Index Fund (Nifty 50) — low-cost large cap exposure
  • 1 International/Sectoral Fund — geographic or sector diversification

Expected pairwise overlap: 5-30% across most pairs. The Flexi Cap may overlap ~40% with the Index Fund — that's acceptable.

When 6+ Funds Is Justified

There are legitimate reasons to hold more than 5 funds, but only if they serve distinct purposes:

  • Tax optimization: Keeping an old ELSS fund (post lock-in) while running SIP in a new one
  • Goal segregation: Separate funds for retirement, child education, and house down payment with different risk profiles
  • Transition periods: Gradually shifting from one fund to another without a lump sum switch

Even in these cases, check that your combined equity holdings don't overlap excessively. Use OverlapIQ to compare all your funds at once.

Signs You Have Too Many Funds

  • 🚩 Two or more funds from the same category (e.g., two Large Caps)
  • 🚩 Pairwise overlap exceeding 50% between any two funds
  • 🚩 The same stock appearing in your top 10 across 3+ funds
  • 🚩 You can't explain why each fund is different from the others
  • 🚩 Total portfolio holds fewer unique stocks than your largest single fund

How to Consolidate Without Losing Money

If you've identified excess overlap, here's a tax-efficient approach to consolidation:

Step 1: Use OverlapIQ to identify which fund pairs overlap the most.

Step 2: Between the overlapping pair, keep the fund with better long-term returns (5-year rolling) and lower expense ratio.

Step 3: Stop the SIP in the fund you're exiting. Don't redeem existing units if you'll trigger STCG tax (holding period < 1 year for equity funds).

Step 4: Redirect the SIP amount to a fund in a different category that your portfolio currently lacks (e.g., if you have no Small Cap exposure).

Step 5: After 1 year, redeem the remaining units from the exited fund to qualify for LTCG treatment (12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh exemption).
