India's REITs in Q1 FY27: Payouts, Growth Drivers and What to Watch in Q2

Six listed REITs distributed ₹3,136 crore in the April-June quarter. Here is what drove the numbers, how much of the growth was organic, where yields stand and what to look for when the next results arrive.

📊 Direct Answer

India's six listed REITs distributed ₹3,136 crore to more than 485,000 unitholders in Q1 FY27, against ₹1,559 crore a year earlier, though that comparison covers four REITs against six. Distribution per unit rose between 5% and 15% where a comparison was given. Growth was led by Global Capability Centre leasing and, for Brookfield and Mindspace, by acquisitions. Yields ranged from 4.8% to 6.3% on 24 September 2026.

Five Themes From the Quarter

1. Global Capability Centres are doing the leasing

Embassy said 81% of the 1.3 million sq ft it leased in the quarter went to GCCs. The market data points the same way: across the top seven cities in the first half of 2026, office absorption was 27.4 million sq ft against 22.2 million sq ft of new completions, average vacancy was about 15%, and GCC leasing rose 22% to a record 19.2 million sq ft (Business Today). Demand that exceeds new supply is what supports rent growth, so this is the single most important driver for the office REITs.

2. Some of the growth was bought

Brookfield's NOI jumped from ₹499 crore to ₹757 crore, but same-store NOI grew 8%, and the gap is acquisitions. Mindspace's 27.8% NOI growth likewise includes new assets. Acquisitions can be good for unitholders if they are bought at attractive yields and funded sensibly, but they are not the same as organic rent growth. When you compare REITs, ask for same-store figures.

3. A new name, and a clean balance sheet

Bagmane Prime Office REIT debuted on 14 May at ₹100 per unit, raising ₹3,405 crore. Its first distribution of ₹1.50 per unit covers a full quarter even though it was listed for only part of it, so it is not a clean run-rate. It reported 98.7% committed occupancy and a loan-to-value (LTV) ratio of 4%, but with six parks in one city it is the least diversified of the six.

4. Money is available, and it costs about 7%

Embassy raised ₹3,045 crore of debt at a blended 7.46%, and Nexus reported a 7.2% cost of debt. Brookfield raised ₹2,600 crore through a QIP in April, which is equity, and RBI has allowed banks to lend to REITs on the same footing as InvITs (Vinod Kothari). Cheap, plentiful funding helps acquisitions. It also means distribution growth now depends on spreads between what assets earn and what debt costs, not just on occupancy.

5. Retail is the quiet diversifier

Nexus Select Trust, the only listed retail REIT, reported tenant consumption of ₹3,850 crore, up 17%, with an 18% LTV and an AAA/Stable rating. Its DPU of ₹2.442 rose 10% year on year. It follows consumer spending, not corporate hiring, which is why it behaves differently from the five office REITs.

A spread of 4.8% to 6.3% means REITs yield roughly what investment-grade bonds do, with the difference being that distributions can rise or fall and unit prices move every day. Business Today also notes that REITs trade at significant valuation multiples and are not equivalent to fixed-income products.

Policy and Tax Backdrop

  • Equity classification. From 1 January 2026, SEBI treats REIT units as equity-related instruments for mutual funds and SIFs, while InvITs remain hybrid. SEBI said REITs could be considered for equity index inclusion only after 1 July 2026.
  • Dividend component. The Taxation and Other Laws (Amendment) Act, 2026 removed the condition that the distributing SPV must not opt for the concessional tax regime, so dividend distributions are exempt in the unitholder's hands. Mondaq reports assent on 17 August 2026 and application from 1 April 2026. Interest, rental income and capital gains are still taxed; see our REIT taxation guide.
  • Lending. Banks can now lend to REITs, as they can to InvITs.

What to Watch in the Q2 FY27 Results

The July-September quarter's results are due over the coming weeks; check each REIT's filing calendar. This is our checklist for reading them, not a forecast.

  • Same-store NOI. Is growth organic, or acquired? Brookfield and Mindspace are the two to read carefully.
  • DPU against guidance. Embassy reaffirmed FY27 DPU guidance of ₹27.0 to ₹28.6. If that is read as a full-year total, Q1's ₹6.31 annualises to about ₹25.2, so later quarters have to be higher. Watch whether Q2 steps up.
  • Bagmane's first run-rate quarter. Compare its next DPU with the ₹1.50 first payout, since Q1 covered a full quarter though the REIT was listed for part of it.
  • Occupancy on a like-for-like definition. Embassy reports by value, others report committed occupancy. Compare each trust with its own previous quarter.
  • Cost of debt. With Embassy at 7.46% and Nexus at 7.2%, a change in refinancing costs flows straight into DPU.
  • Supply and GCC demand. Is absorption still ahead of completions, and is GCC leasing holding up?
  • Brookfield's BKC deal. Does the proposed 50:50 acquisition complete, and how is it funded?

What This Means for an Investor

Nothing here is a call to buy or sell any unit. The quarter shows a sector with strong office demand, plenty of capital and rising distributions, and also one where headline growth mixes organic and acquired income and where yields are only a little above bonds. Frequently Asked Questions

How much did Indian REITs distribute in Q1 FY27?

The six listed REITs distributed ₹3,136 crore in the April-June 2026 quarter to more than 485,000 unitholders, against ₹1,559 crore a year earlier. The earlier figure covers four REITs and the latest covers six, so the increase is not like-for-like. They have distributed over ₹34,800 crore since inception.

Which REIT grew its distribution the most in Q1 FY27?

Mindspace Business Parks REIT reported the largest year-on-year DPU increase among those that gave a comparison, at 15.2% to ₹6.67, helped by acquisitions. Nexus Select Trust's DPU rose 10% to ₹2.442 and Embassy's rose 9% to ₹6.31. Brookfield's release did not give a year-ago comparison, and Knowledge Realty Trust's 5% is against the previous quarter.

What are REIT yields in India now?

On 24 September 2026, distribution yields ranged from 4.8% for Mindspace to 6.3% for Brookfield, with a median of 5.8% across five REITs, according to Business Today. Bagmane was not included. Yield changes with unit prices, so calculate it from the latest distribution and the current price.

When are the Q2 FY27 REIT results?

Quarterly results for the July-September period are normally published over the following several weeks. We do not have confirmed dates, so check each REIT's investor-relations page or exchange filing calendar.

People Also Ask

Why did REIT payouts double?
Mostly because the count of listed REITs rose from four to six. Knowledge Realty Trust and Bagmane were not in the year-ago total. Per-unit distributions grew at a more modest pace, between 5% and 15% where a comparison was given.
What is same-store NOI?
Net operating income from assets the REIT owned in both periods being compared. It strips out the effect of acquisitions, so it shows organic growth. Brookfield's NOI rose far faster than its same-store NOI of 8%.
Are REIT distributions guaranteed?
No. They depend on rent collected from tenants and on costs and debt service. REITs must distribute at least 90% of distributable cash flow, but the amount varies by quarter and unit prices fluctuate.
