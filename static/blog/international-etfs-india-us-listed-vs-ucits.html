International ETFs from India: US-Listed vs Irish UCITS (Fees, Estate Tax, Which Fits)

ETFs are the simplest way to own the world, but they come in two boxes: US-listed and Irish UCITS. Here are eight popular ones, what really changes between the boxes, and a calculator for the estate-tax question.

📊 Direct Answer

Popular international ETFs for Indians come as US-listed funds (VOO, VTI, QQQ, VT) or Irish UCITS funds (CSPX, CNDX, SWDA, VWRA). They hold the same big companies. US-listed ETFs are cheaper and easier to buy but are generally exposed to US estate tax above USD 60,000; Irish UCITS ETFs are generally treated as non-US property but cost a little more.

The short answer

An international ETF is a ready-made basket of foreign stocks that you buy as one item. For Indians the popular ones come in two boxes: US-listed ETFs such as VOO, VTI, QQQ and VT, and Irish-domiciled UCITS ETFs such as CSPX, CNDX, SWDA and VWRA. Inside, they hold the same famous companies. What changes is the box: the US estate-tax exposure, the dividend-tax layers, the yearly fee and how easily you can buy.

The two dividend rates are not directly comparable

The 25% and the 15% are taken at different layers. The 25% on a US-listed ETF can be claimed as a credit when you pay Indian tax. The 15% inside an Irish fund cannot. That is why this guide compares costs with a calculator rather than just two percentages. For the estate side, read our US estate tax guide.

Does the estate-tax risk outweigh the extra cost?

Two different things are being compared. The extra yearly cost of the Irish box is certain: you pay it through slightly lower growth. The US estate tax is contingent: it only arises if you die holding more than USD 60,000 of US assets. Use the calculator to see both on your own numbers.

How to read the calculator

The default extra cost (0.28% a year) is the gap INDmoney reports between a US S&P 500 ETF and its Irish UCITS twin from 2021 to 2025, before Indian tax. That gap includes the 15% withholding inside the Irish fund, which is a separate layer from the 25% you can claim back on a US ETF, so it may overstate the real difference. Try 0.10% to see how the answer moves. The USD 60,000 line is a fixed dollar figure, so a growing portfolio can cross it even if you never add money.

Which box fits which situation

Not advice. These are the ways the trade-off is commonly described.

📏

Small and simple

Under USD 60,000 of total US-listed holdings the estate rule is generally not triggered. US-listed ETFs then have the widest support and the lowest fees. Re-check as you grow.

📈

Growing past USD 60,000

Irish UCITS ETFs are the commonly discussed alternative. Run the calculator. Selling what you already hold can create Indian tax, so check that first.

🌍

One fund for everything

World ETFs (VT, SWDA, VWRA) hold thousands of companies, yet the same big names lead. Wide is not the same as different from your S&P 500 fund, so check the overlap.

Five mistakes to avoid

  1. Thinking two ETF names mean two portfolios. An S&P 500 ETF in a US box and in an Irish box is the same basket.
  2. Comparing only the headline fee. INDmoney says tracking difference, not the headline fee alone, is what to compare.
  3. Buying a distributing class by mistake. Distributing funds pay cash dividends that are generally taxed in India at your rate. The accumulating classes in this guide reinvest them.
  4. Ignoring the listing and currency. The same fund can trade in several currencies. Check the line, the currency and the live price gap before you buy.
  5. Switching in a hurry. Selling a US-listed ETF to buy a UCITS one can create Indian capital gains tax and paperwork. Work out the cost first.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best international ETF for Indian investors?

There is no single best one. It depends on what you want: US large companies (S&P 500 ETFs), US tech (Nasdaq-100), the whole US market, or the whole world. The box matters too: US-listed ETFs are cheaper and easier to buy, while Irish UCITS ETFs are generally outside US estate tax. This guide compares eight examples and is not a recommendation.

Is VOO or CSPX better for an Indian investor?

They follow the same index, the S&P 500. VOO is US-listed with a 0.03% fee. CSPX is an Irish UCITS ETF with a 0.07% fee, accumulates dividends, and is generally treated as non-US property for US estate tax. Which is better depends on how much you hold, how long, and your tax position, so run the calculator and ask a chartered accountant.

Do Irish UCITS ETFs avoid US estate tax?

INDmoney says Irish and Luxembourg UCITS ETFs are generally treated as non-US property, even when they hold US companies, while US-domiciled ETFs are generally treated as US property. The legal domicile of the fund matters, not the ticker. Confirm your own situation with a professional.

What does an accumulating ETF mean?

An accumulating ETF reinvests the dividends inside the fund instead of paying them to you. You get no cash dividend, and in the framework INDmoney describes the tax is deferred until you sell, not removed. Distributing ETFs pay cash dividends, which are generally taxed in India at your rate.

Can I buy UCITS ETFs through Indian apps?

INDmoney says UCITS ETFs are available on its platform and can usually be bought in fractions from about USD 1.01. Platform lists change, so check inside the app. Both US-listed and UCITS investing from India generally go through the Liberalised Remittance Scheme, which INDmoney says allows up to USD 250,000 per financial year.

How much do an S&P 500 ETF and a Nasdaq-100 ETF overlap?

Using the biggest 20 holdings in the OverlapIQ analyzer, at least 42% of the combined money sits in the same companies, and the real figure is higher because the smaller holdings overlap too. The same index in a US box and an Irish box is 100% the same portfolio.

People Also Ask

Is a world ETF more diversified than an S&P 500 ETF?
It holds many more companies and countries, but the biggest names are the same US companies. In this data NVIDIA, Apple and Microsoft are the top three in the S&P 500, MSCI World, FTSE All-World and total world ETFs alike.
Do I pay Indian tax on these ETFs?
Yes. INDmoney says Indian capital-gains treatment is broadly similar for both boxes, and foreign assets must be disclosed in Schedule FA. Rates and holding-period rules change, so check current rules or ask a chartered accountant.
Why not just buy an Indian international fund?
You can, and some people prefer to. But many Indian international funds are closed to new money and some international ETFs trade above their value. Our 3-step guide explains the four ways in.
