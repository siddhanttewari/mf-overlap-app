How to Invest in GIFT City via LRS: Step-by-Step Guide

The process is more paperwork than complexity — here's exactly what happens between deciding to invest and seeing your GIFT City units show up.

📊 Key Point

You invest in GIFT City using your existing bank account and the RBI's Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS) — no new domestic account needed. The core steps are: pick a product, complete the platform's KYC, submit Form A2 and an LRS declaration to your bank, and let the remittance process through. Typical turnaround is a few business days.

Before You Start

Confirm three things before initiating any remittance: the product you're considering is open to resident Indians (not all GIFT City products are — some are NRI/foreign-investor only), you have enough unused LRS headroom for the financial year (your USD 250,000 annual cap is shared across all remittance purposes, not just investing), and you understand the product's minimum investment and any lock-in or redemption terms.

Step-by-Step Process

  1. Choose your GIFT City product and platform. Whether it's an outbound retail fund, a UDR platform, or a Global Access Platform, confirm eligibility for resident Indians and check the minimum investment.
  2. Complete KYC with the GIFT City platform. This typically requires your PAN, address proof, and bank account details — separate from any KYC you've already done for domestic mutual funds or brokers.
  3. Initiate the LRS remittance with your bank. Submit Form A2 (the standard remittance application) along with the LRS declaration, specifying "investment" as the purpose and providing the GIFT City entity's receiving account details, usually supplied by the platform during onboarding.
  4. Bank processes the remittance. Your bank converts INR to USD at the prevailing exchange rate and transfers the funds, subject to its own LRS compliance checks — this is the step most likely to introduce delays if documentation is incomplete.
  5. Funds reflect in your GIFT City account. Once received, the platform allots units or executes your investment instruction, and you should receive a confirmation.
  6. Track and report the investment. Keep records of the remittance (amount, date, purpose) for your own reference, and disclose the holding in Schedule FA of your income tax return for the relevant assessment year.

Documents You'll Need

  • Form A2 — the standard application for outward remittance under LRS, usually available from your bank's net banking portal or branch.
  • PAN card — required for both the LRS remittance and the GIFT City platform's KYC.
  • LRS declaration — a self-declaration confirming the purpose of remittance and that you haven't exceeded your annual USD 250,000 limit across all purposes.
  • Bank account details of the receiving GIFT City entity — provided by the platform once your account there is set up.

Exact document requirements can vary slightly bank to bank — check with your specific bank's forex/remittance desk before initiating.

Common Issues and How to Avoid Them

Incomplete LRS headroom tracking. If you've already used part of your USD 250,000 limit this financial year for travel, education, or other investments, your bank will check this — know your remaining headroom before initiating to avoid a rejected or partial remittance.

Mismatched KYC details. Ensure the name, PAN, and address on your bank's LRS documentation exactly match what you've submitted to the GIFT City platform — mismatches are a common cause of processing delays.

Exchange rate timing. Since your rupees convert to USD at the time of remittance, the exact rate isn't something you control precisely — if timing matters to you, ask your bank about their forex conversion process and cut-off times.

After You've Invested

Keep the remittance confirmation and any statements from the GIFT City platform — you'll need these for your Schedule FA disclosure at tax filing time, and for your own tracking of cost basis in USD terms (relevant for calculating gains when you eventually redeem). If you plan to invest further in future years, remember your USD 250,000 limit resets each financial year, so plan any additional GIFT City allocations alongside your other overseas remittance needs.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does an LRS remittance to GIFT City take?

Typically a few business days from submitting your Form A2 and LRS declaration to funds reflecting in the GIFT City platform's account, though timing varies by bank and can take longer if additional compliance checks are triggered.

Do I need a new bank account to invest in GIFT City?

No — you use your existing savings account and your bank's LRS remittance process. You don't need to open a new domestic account, though the GIFT City platform itself will typically require you to complete its own onboarding and KYC.

What documents does my bank need for a GIFT City LRS remittance?

Typically Form A2 (the standard LRS remittance application), a PAN card, and the LRS declaration confirming the purpose of remittance and that you haven't exceeded your USD 250,000 annual limit. Specific document requirements can vary slightly by bank.

People Also Ask

Can I invest in GIFT City directly without going through a platform?
No — you invest through the specific fund, brokerage, or Global Access Platform operating in GIFT City, similar to how you'd invest in a domestic mutual fund through an AMC or broker rather than directly with the regulator.
Can I set up a recurring/SIP-style investment into GIFT City?
It depends on the platform — some GIFT City products support recurring investments, but each remittance still needs to go through the LRS process individually, so check with your specific platform on whether they support a streamlined recurring option.
What happens to my GIFT City investment if I use up my full LRS limit elsewhere?
You won't be able to remit further funds to GIFT City (or for any other LRS purpose) until the next financial year, when your USD 250,000 allowance resets.
