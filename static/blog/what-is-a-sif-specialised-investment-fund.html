What is a SIF (Specialised Investment Fund)? Complete Guide for Indian Investors

SEBI created a new investment category in 2025 that sits between mutual funds and PMS — with a ₹10 lakh entry ticket and far more flexibility to go short. Here's what a SIF actually is, who it's for, and what to check before you put money in.

SIF assets under management grew from roughly ₹2,010 crore in October 2025 to around ₹9,711 crore by February 2026, with hybrid long-short strategies accounting for about 76% of that AUM. By mid-2026, roughly 27-30 live SIF schemes were on offer from 13+ AMCs — the category is still small, but growing fast.

What is a SIF?

A Specialised Investment Fund (SIF) is a SEBI-regulated investment product, introduced via the SEBI (Mutual Funds) (Third Amendment) Regulations, 2024, and effective from April 1, 2025. It operates under the same mutual fund regulatory framework — daily NAV disclosure, SEBI oversight, AMC-run — but with meaningfully more flexibility than a traditional mutual fund scheme.

The core idea: give AMCs a licensed way to offer strategies that were previously only available through PMS (Portfolio Management Services) or AIFs (Alternative Investment Funds) — including the ability to take short positions using derivatives, rotate concentrated sector bets, and run hybrid long-short books — while keeping the lower entry ticket and regulatory guardrails of the mutual fund ecosystem.

Every AMC offering SIFs must run the business under a separate brand identity, distinct from its regular mutual fund platform — for example, DSP's SIF brand is Endurance, 360 ONE's is Dyna, Union MF's is Arthaya, and Mirae Asset's is Platinum.

Why Did SEBI Create SIFs?

Before SIFs, an investor with, say, ₹15-30 lakh who wanted more sophisticated strategies than a plain-vanilla equity fund had two real options: pay for a PMS (typically ₹50 lakh minimum) or an AIF (typically ₹1 crore minimum), or stay within the constraints of standard mutual fund regulations, which restrict short-selling and derivative use.

SIFs were designed to fill that gap — a middle tier with a ₹10 lakh minimum that's high enough to signal "sophisticated investor" but far more accessible than PMS or AIF, run inside the same regulatory perimeter as mutual funds rather than the lighter-touch AIF framework.

Key Features of a SIF

₹10 Lakh Minimum, Per PAN

The minimum investment is ₹10 lakh per investor, aggregated at the PAN level across all SIF strategies offered by a single AMC — not per scheme. So ₹10 lakh spread across two SIF strategies from the same AMC satisfies the minimum; you don't need ₹10 lakh in each. SEBI-defined accredited investors are exempt from this threshold entirely. Neither AMCs nor distributors can lower this minimum.

Derivatives and Short Positions

Unlike regular mutual fund schemes, SIFs can use derivatives more actively — including taking short positions — subject to SEBI-mandated limits on net short exposure. This is what allows long-short and hedged strategies that simply aren't possible in a standard equity or hybrid mutual fund.

Stricter Manager Eligibility

SEBI requires SIFs to be run by senior investment professionals with a strong track record — either a CIO with at least 10 years of fund management experience and average AUM of ₹5,000+ crore (plus a fund manager with 3+ years and ₹500+ crore average AUM), or an equivalent alternate route. The intent is to keep these more flexible strategies in experienced hands.

SIP, SWP and STP Are Allowed

Despite the higher minimum, AMCs can offer systematic investment (SIP), systematic withdrawal (SWP) and systematic transfer (STP) options in SIFs, as long as the ₹10 lakh aggregate threshold is maintained.

The Types of SIF Strategies

SEBI's framework allows SIFs across equity-oriented, debt-oriented and hybrid categories. The strategies seen in the market so far include:

Strategy TypeWhat it does
Equity long-shortTakes both long and short positions in equities to manage market direction risk
Sector rotation long-shortRotates concentrated bets across a limited number of sectors (e.g. up to 4), going short to hedge as a sector's trend turns
Hybrid long-shortCombines equity, debt and derivatives; SEBI mandates a minimum 25% equity and 25% debt exposure, with up to 25% short exposure allowed. This is the dominant strategy by AUM so far.
Debt long-shortApplies long-short techniques within fixed income, aiming to manage interest-rate and credit risk more actively than a plain debt fund

Hybrid long-short strategies account for roughly 76% of total SIF AUM as of early 2026, reflecting where investor demand has concentrated so far.

SIF vs Mutual Fund vs PMS

Mutual Fund

Minimum: ₹100-500 (SIP)

Shorting: Not allowed

Regulation: SEBI MF rules

Best for: Most retail investors

SIF

Minimum: ₹10 lakh (per PAN)

Shorting: Allowed, within limits

Regulation: SEBI MF framework, separate brand

Best for: HNIs wanting more flexibility without a full PMS ticket

PMS / AIF

Minimum: ₹50 lakh (PMS) / ₹1 crore (AIF)

Shorting: Allowed, wide latitude

Regulation: SEBI PMS/AIF rules

Best for: UHNIs, highly customised mandates

The practical distinction: a mutual fund gives you low-cost, highly regulated, long-only exposure. A PMS or AIF gives you a largely customised, high-cost mandate with wide strategic latitude. A SIF sits in between — standardised (not customised to you individually, unlike PMS) but with more strategic tools than a mutual fund, at a fraction of the PMS/AIF entry ticket.

Who Should Consider a SIF?

A SIF may be worth evaluating if: you can comfortably commit ₹10 lakh+ to a single strategy category without it dominating your portfolio, you understand derivatives and long-short mechanics well enough to evaluate the strategy (not just the past few months of returns), and you're looking to diversify beyond plain long-only equity and debt exposure.

A SIF is probably not the right fit if: ₹10 lakh represents a large share of your net worth, you're drawn in primarily by short-term performance numbers (most live SIFs have only 3-8 months of track record as of mid-2026), or you're not clear on how the specific strategy generates returns and manages downside.

Risks to Know Before Investing

Very limited track record. SIFs are a new category — most live schemes have only a few months of performance history. Early returns, especially over 3-8 month windows, are not a reliable indicator of how a long-short or sector-rotation strategy performs across a full market cycle.

Complexity risk. Long-short and derivative-heavy strategies are harder to evaluate than a plain equity fund's stock list. Underperformance can come from the short leg, the long leg, or timing — not always obvious from the outside.

Liquidity structure varies. Some SIFs are open-ended, others operate on an interval or minimum-redemption-frequency basis. Check the Scheme Information Document (SID) for redemption terms before investing.

Concentration risk. Sector-rotation and hybrid long-short strategies can run concentrated positions in a handful of sectors or names, which is by design — but it means volatility can be materially higher than a diversified equity fund.

How SIFs Are Taxed

SIF taxation depends on the underlying portfolio composition of the specific fund, not a single blanket rule for the category:

Equity-oriented SIFs are typically taxed like equity mutual funds — LTCG at 12.5% after 12 months (above the exemption threshold) and STCG at 20% within 12 months.

Hybrid and debt-oriented SIFs can be taxed differently depending on their actual equity/debt mix, since the classification follows the fund's holdings rather than its label. This makes it important to check each scheme's SID and category classification individually before assuming a tax treatment.

Frequently Asked Questions

When were SIFs introduced in India?

SEBI notified the framework via the SEBI (Mutual Funds) (Third Amendment) Regulations, 2024, in late 2024, with the category becoming effective from April 1, 2025. AMCs began launching live SIF schemes and NFOs through the rest of 2025 and into 2026.

Can I invest in a SIF through the same app I use for mutual funds?

It varies by platform. Some AMCs run a dedicated SIF platform separate from their regular mutual fund site, and third-party investment apps are still rolling out SIF support unevenly. Check the specific AMC's SIF website or AMFI's SIF section for current availability before assuming your regular app supports it.

Is ₹10 lakh a one-time minimum or an ongoing requirement?

It's the minimum investment threshold at the PAN level, aggregated across an AMC's SIF strategies. Once invested, you're not required to maintain a running ₹10 lakh balance forever, but the initial commitment across an AMC's SIF offerings must meet that bar.

People Also Ask

Are SIFs regulated by SEBI?
Yes. SIFs operate under the same SEBI mutual fund regulatory framework — daily NAV disclosure, AMC-level oversight, and SEBI-mandated reporting — rather than the lighter-touch AIF regime.
Can a SIF go fully short on the market?
No. SEBI caps net short exposure — for example, hybrid long-short strategies are capped at 25% short exposure alongside minimum 25% equity and 25% debt allocations. SIFs can hedge and go tactically short, but they aren't unconstrained short funds.
How many SIF schemes are available in India right now?
Roughly 27-30 live or NFO-stage SIF schemes from 13+ AMCs as of mid-2026, and the number is growing as more AMCs receive SIF licences. Check AMFI's SIF section for the current live count.
