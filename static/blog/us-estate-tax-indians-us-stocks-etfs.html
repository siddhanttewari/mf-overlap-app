US Estate Tax for Indians: Do You Need to Worry About Your US Stocks and ETFs?

A US tax on what you leave behind can apply to Indians who hold US stocks and ETFs. Here is the USD 60,000 rule in plain English, what it could cost, and what people do about it.

📊 Direct Answer

If you are not a US citizen or resident, a US estate tax return (Form 706-NA) is generally required when your US-situated assets, which include US stocks and US-listed ETFs, are worth more than USD 60,000 at death. Tax starts at 18% and steps up to 40%; a credit of up to USD 13,000 covers the first slice. As an illustration, USD 100,000 of US-listed holdings would carry about USD 10,800 of tax before deductions. Irish-domiciled UCITS ETFs are generally treated as non-US. This is general information, not tax advice.

The short answer

It is a US tax on what you leave behind, not on what you own today. If you are not a US citizen or resident and the US-listed stocks and ETFs you hold are worth more than USD 60,000 when you die, your family may have to file a US estate tax return and pay US tax, even though you live in India and bought through an Indian app.

⏳

STEP 1

When

Only at death. Holding US shares today does not trigger a bill.

🇺🇸

STEP 2

What counts

US stocks and US-listed ETFs, added up across all your apps.

📝

STEP 3

Who acts

Your executor files Form 706-NA, normally within 9 months.

🧒 Like you are 5

Imagine you keep toys in a friend's house in another country. Nothing happens while you are alive. But if the toys are worth a lot when you are gone, that country's tax office may ask for a share before your family can take them home. Toys kept in your own house, or in a box that is legally yours at home, are not affected.

What exactly is the rule?

According to the IRS, an executor of a nonresident who is not a US citizen must file Form 706-NA if the US-situated assets are worth more than USD 60,000 on the date of death. The test uses the full value of those assets, not what is left after deductions. Stock of US-organised companies counts as US property even if the certificates are held abroad.

Under that threshold there is generally nothing to file and nothing to pay. Over it, tax starts at 18% and climbs to 40% on very large amounts. A credit of up to USD 13,000 means the first USD 60,000 is effectively covered. Note: the credit does not make the next dollar tax-free, so going a little above USD 60,000 does not mean a 40% bill. Four ways people handle it

None of these is advice. They are the choices commonly discussed, with the catch for each.

📏

Stay under the line

Keep total US-listed holdings below USD 60,000. Simple, but you must watch it as your investments grow and the dollar moves.

🇮🇪

Use Irish UCITS funds

Buy a London-listed Irish-domiciled ETF instead of a US-listed one. Generally outside this rule, with different costs and dividend tax.

🇮🇳

Use Indian international funds

Units of an Indian fund are not US shares, but most are shut to new money and some ETFs trade at a premium. Check our 3-step guide.

Do not switch only because of this article

Selling US-listed holdings can create Indian capital gains tax and paperwork. A UCITS ETF can cost slightly more each year and is taxed differently on dividends. The right answer depends on your total amount, age, family and plans. A chartered accountant with cross-border experience can run the numbers for your case.

A simple "if something happens to me" checklist

Whatever you decide, a clear paper trail saves your family months. This is the one part that costs nothing.

  • A list of every account that holds foreign assets: app, email, account number.
  • Nominee details filled in on each platform.
  • A will that mentions foreign assets, made with a lawyer who knows cross-border cases.
  • Statements and dividend records saved yearly (you also need them for Form 67 and Schedule FA).
  • One trusted person who knows where the list is.
  • A yearly reminder to re-add your total US-listed holdings against USD 60,000.

Five mistakes to avoid

  1. Counting each app separately. The USD 60,000 line applies to everything you hold in the US, combined.
  2. Assuming a 40% bill the moment you cross the line. Rates are stepped, and the USD 13,000 credit covers the first slice.
  3. Assuming the income-tax treaty helps. Per INDmoney, India is not on the IRS list of countries with a US estate or gift tax treaty.
  4. Ignoring it because it only matters at death. Your family bears the work, and holdings may be held back until the return is dealt with.
  5. Selling everything in a panic. Check the Indian tax cost of selling first.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do Indians have to pay US estate tax on US stocks?

Possibly. If you are not a US citizen or resident and your US-situated assets, including US stocks and US-listed ETFs, are worth more than USD 60,000 on the date of death, the executor generally has to file Form 706-NA, and US tax can be due. Below USD 60,000 there is generally no filing and no tax.

What is the USD 60,000 limit for US estate tax?

It is the filing threshold for nonresidents who are not US citizens. It applies to the full value of US-situated assets at death. A credit of up to USD 13,000 covers the tax on the first USD 60,000 of taxable estate, so tax is only due on amounts above that.

Are US-listed ETFs covered by US estate tax?

Generally yes. INDmoney explains that US-domiciled ETFs are generally treated as US property for a non-US investor, while Irish or Luxembourg corporate UCITS ETFs are generally treated as non-US. The legal issuer and domicile matter more than the ticker or listing venue. Confirm your own case with a chartered accountant.

Is there an India-US estate tax treaty?

INDmoney says India does not appear on the IRS list of countries with a US estate or gift tax treaty, and that the income-tax treaty should not be assumed to give estate-tax protection. Treaty positions can change, so confirm with a professional.

How much would the tax be on USD 100,000 of US stocks?

As an illustration, using the published rate table with no deductions: tentative tax of USD 23,800 less the USD 13,000 credit is about USD 10,800, around 10.8% of the holding. Real estates can have deductions, so a professional calculation is needed.

When is Form 706-NA due?

The IRS says Form 706-NA is due within 9 months after the date of death, unless an extension was granted. Form 4768 can request an automatic 6-month extension.

People Also Ask

Does this apply while I am alive?
No. This is an estate tax, charged on assets left behind at death. Holding US stocks or ETFs while alive does not trigger it. Other taxes (dividend withholding, capital gains) apply while you are alive.
Do Indian international mutual funds trigger US estate tax?
The sources we read apply the rule to US-organised stock and US-domiciled funds you own directly. They do not address units of an Indian fund that holds US assets. Ask a chartered accountant about your specific fund.
Is a Will enough?
A Will helps your family claim assets, but it does not remove a US filing duty. Many people also keep a list of foreign accounts and nominee details. Speak to a lawyer who handles cross-border estates.
