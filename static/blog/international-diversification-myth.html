Is Your International Diversification Really Just 5 US Tech Stocks?

You hold a Nasdaq-100 fund, an S&P 500 fund and maybe a world index fund. Three funds, three labels. But open the boxes and the same companies keep turning up.

📊 Direct Answer

In our three-fund illustration (40% Nasdaq-100, 30% S&P 500, 30% MSCI ACWI), about 35% of the combined portfolio sits in five companies: NVIDIA, Apple, Microsoft, Amazon and Alphabet. Real Indian funds show the same pattern: at least 42% overlap between a Nasdaq-100 and an S&P 500 fund, and 100% between two Nasdaq-100 funds.

Three funds, three fund houses and three expense ratios, but about 35% of the combined portfolio sits in the same five companies. Nothing is wrong with owning NVIDIA, Apple, Microsoft, Amazon or Alphabet. The problem is thinking three funds gave you three different bets.

Why Every Index Holds the Same Giants

All three indices weight companies by market value, so the biggest companies get the biggest slices everywhere. In the MSCI ACWI, the ten biggest companies make up 25.40% of the whole index, and nine of the ten are American: NVIDIA 5.11%, Apple 4.76%, Microsoft 3.52%, Amazon 2.35%, Alphabet class A 1.97%, TSMC 1.87%, Alphabet class C 1.55%, Meta 1.55%, Broadcom 1.55% and Micron 1.17%. The United States is 64.21% of MSCI ACWI, so a "world" fund is mostly one country.

Where Real Variety Comes From

Spreading across regions changes the picture, because other markets are less top-heavy and hold different companies:

  • Developed markets outside the US. MSCI EAFE's ten biggest companies are only 14.20% of the index (ASML 3.28%, HSBC 1.59%, Roche 1.37%, Shell 1.28%, Novartis 1.22%), and Japan is 24.87% of it.
  • Japan and Taiwan funds. In our data, Nippon India Japan Equity Fund (top 20 holdings, 30 Sep 2026) and Nippon India Taiwan Equity Fund (top 10, 31 Jul 2026) share no disclosed top holdings with the Nasdaq-100 tracker. The Taiwan fund's top holding was MediaTek at 7.34%, and TSMC was not in its top 10.
  • Emerging markets. MSCI Emerging Markets is 28.94% Taiwan, 21.40% South Korea, 19.80% China and 10.65% India. Note that information technology is 43.78% of it and TSMC is its largest holding at 15.63%, so it is not free of the same chip theme, and its India weight overlaps your Indian funds.

Availability is the catch

Most Indian international funds are closed to fresh money because of the USD 7 billion industry cap. Value Research listed only 4 as open or reopening on 25 September 2026, and none was US-focused. Our International Investing Hub explains the four doors in and what is open.

How to Check Your Own Portfolio

  1. List everything with foreign exposure: international funds, index funds, flexi cap funds that hold overseas shares, and direct US stocks or ETFs.
  2. Look inside each one, not at its name. A "US Bluechip" fund can look very different from the S&P 500 (ICICI Prudential US Bluechip's biggest holding at 30 Sep 2026 was Microsoft at 2.97%).
  3. Add up the repeats. If direct NVIDIA sits on top of a Nasdaq-100 fund, that is the same bet twice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do a Nasdaq-100 fund and an S&P 500 fund overlap?

Yes. From the top 20 holdings alone, Motilal Oswal Nasdaq 100 FoF and Motilal Oswal S&P 500 Index Fund overlap by at least 42.0% in our October 2026 data. The real figure is higher because the rest of the holdings also share names. Two Nasdaq-100 funds overlap by 100%, since they copy the same index.

Is a world index fund enough diversification?

It spreads you across thousands of companies, but the US is 64.21% of MSCI ACWI and 72.94% of MSCI World, and its ten biggest companies are 25.40% of ACWI. Adding a separate US fund mostly adds more of the same US giants.

Is buying US stocks directly diversification if I hold international funds?

Only if you buy different companies. Buying NVIDIA or Apple directly on top of a Nasdaq-100 or S&P 500 fund adds to a holding you already have. Direct investing also brings LRS, 20% TCS above Rs 10 lakh a year, Schedule FA reporting and possible US estate tax exposure above USD 60,000.

Can I still buy international funds in India?

Mostly not. The USD 7 billion industry limit has been full since 2022 and most funds are closed to fresh money. Value Research listed 4 funds as open or reopening on 25 September 2026, none US-focused, and the list changes often. Check the fund house before you plan around any fund.

People Also Ask

How much of my portfolio should be international?
There is no single right number. It depends on your goals, time horizon, tax position and how much currency and single-country risk you can accept. Treat any percentage in an article as a starting point for a conversation with a registered adviser, not a target.
Is rupee depreciation a reason to invest internationally?
Currency moves go both ways. When the rupee weakens against the dollar, the rupee value of a US holding rises; when it strengthens, the value falls. It adds volatility rather than acting as a guaranteed hedge, and it is a poor reason on its own to invest abroad.
Should I use mutual funds or buy foreign stocks directly?
It depends on access and rules. Indian international funds are mostly closed; direct investing through LRS is open but carries TCS, Schedule FA and estate tax considerations. Our guide on GIFT City vs direct US investing compares the routes side by side.
