GIFT City vs Investing Directly in US Stocks: Which Route Suits an Indian Resident?

Both routes use your annual LRS limit and the same TCS, so the real difference is what you own, how dividends and gains are taxed, and whether the US estate tax can reach your portfolio. Here is how to compare them.

📊 Direct Answer

For a resident Indian, GIFT City uses the same USD 250,000 LRS limit, the same 20% TCS above ₹10 lakh and the same Schedule FA disclosure as buying US stocks directly. Direct investing gives full choice but exposes US shares worth more than USD 60,000 to US estate tax. A GIFT City fund gives fewer choices, adds a fund fee and is taxed according to its structure. Most Indian international mutual funds are closed to fresh money because of the USD 7 billion industry cap.

The Short Answer

For a resident Indian, GIFT City is not a way around the rules of investing abroad. It uses the same LRS limit of USD 250,000 a year, the same 20% TCS above ₹10 lakh, and the same Schedule FA disclosure as buying US shares directly. What changes is what you hold: with the direct route you own US securities and pick them yourself, while in GIFT City you usually own units of a fund that picks them for you.

That difference matters in three places: how much choice you have, how dividends and gains are taxed, and whether the US estate tax can reach your holdings. The tax-free headlines about GIFT City are about non-residents; our guide on GIFT City for NRIs vs residents explains that split.

The Direct Route, Step by Step

You open an account with a platform that can send rupees abroad under LRS (an Indian app with a US partner broker, or an international broker), complete its KYC and a W-8BEN form, remit dollars, and buy. Three costs and rules show up in practice.

  • TCS. If you send ₹25 lakh in a financial year and nothing else goes abroad, TCS is 20% of the ₹15 lakh above the ₹10 lakh threshold, which is ₹3 lakh. It is not an extra tax, since you claim it as credit when you file, but it is cash out of your pocket in the meantime.
  • Dividends. The US withholds 25% under the India-US treaty (some guides cite up to 30% where treaty paperwork is missing). India taxes the gross dividend. On a USD 100 dividend you receive USD 75; if your slab rate were 30% (before surcharge and cess), India's tax on the gross is USD 30 and you claim USD 25 back through Form 67 and Schedule TR, so you owe about USD 5 more in India. The credit is capped at the Indian tax on that income.
  • Capital gains. Gains are computed in rupees, so currency moves count. Suppose you buy USD 10,000 of an ETF at ₹84 per dollar (₹8.40 lakh) and sell it after three years for USD 14,000 at ₹90 (₹12.60 lakh). The gain is ₹4.20 lakh and long-term tax at 12.5% is ₹52,500, before surcharge and cess. Motilal Oswal notes that conversion uses the SBI TT buying rate on the last day of the month before the transaction.

Every year, you must also report these holdings in Schedule FA for the calendar year, whether or not you sold anything. See our Schedule FA guide for how the disclosure works and why the Black Money Act penalties make it worth getting right.

The Risk Most Direct Investors Miss: US Estate Tax

US estate tax applies to non-residents too

The IRS treats stock in US corporations as US-situated property, even if held through a nominee, and requires the executor of a non-resident's estate to file Form 706-NA if US-situated assets are worth more than USD 60,000 at death. India and the US have no estate tax treaty, so the income-tax treaty does not help (INDmoney's summary). Rates can go up to 40%.

In practice, this means a portfolio of US stocks and US-listed ETFs that has grown past about USD 60,000 (about ₹54 lakh at an illustrative ₹90 to the dollar) can create an estate-filing and tax problem for your family. Owners sometimes manage it by spreading holdings across family members, who each have the same threshold, or by choosing instruments that are not US-situs. Ireland-domiciled ETFs are often mentioned, but whether they are open to you depends on your broker and the treaty position, so treat that as a question for a cross-border tax adviser, not a plan.

This is the clearest structural difference with a fund route, because a fund unit is not a share of a US corporation. We cannot tell you how the IRS would treat a particular GIFT City fund, since that depends on the vehicle, so ask the fund manager for a written view before relying on it.

The GIFT City Route

GIFT City funds are regulated by IFSCA, run in US dollars and are open to residents through LRS. Retail outbound funds put your money into global equities; our list of GIFT City funds and platforms shows what exists, and our LRS walkthrough covers the remittance. What you gain and what you give up:

  • You gain professional selection, no need to hold US shares in your own name, and a route that does not depend on the Indian overseas fund cap.
  • You give up choice, since the menu is a few funds rather than thousands of securities, and you add a fund fee on top of any ETF costs inside it.
  • You inherit short track records, because most GIFT City funds launched between 2022 and 2025.
  • You do not gain a tax break. A resident still pays Indian tax, still gets charged TCS and still files Schedule FA. The exemptions you may read about, including exemption of gains on eligible IFSC fund units, apply to non-residents.

Why Indian International Funds Are Not the Easy Alternative

An Indian international fund needs no LRS and no TCS, which makes it the simplest route. The problem is capacity. The industry crossed the USD 7 billion overseas limit in January 2022, and since then each fund house can invest abroad only up to the headroom it had at the start of February 2022. Value Research counted 66 international funds on 2 July 2026, of which 54 no longer accepted fresh money and 12 accepted new SIPs. A fund reopens only when investors redeem or its overseas holdings lose value. If you hold one already, you hold something scarce.

Before you pay a premium for US exposure, check how much of it you already have: many Indian equity funds hold overseas stocks, and your large-cap and flexi-cap funds already own the Indian arms of global businesses. Our piece on the international diversification myth looks at that.

Which Route Fits Which Situation

This is a way to frame the choice, not a recommendation for any fund or platform.

  • You want specific US stocks or low-cost index ETFs, and your US holdings will stay small. The direct route gives full choice. Keep the estate-tax threshold in mind as the account grows.
  • You expect to hold well above USD 60,000 in the US over time. Ask a cross-border adviser about the estate-tax position first, and compare a fund route.
  • You want someone else to pick and rebalance, and accept fewer choices. A GIFT City outbound fund or a still-open Indian international fund fits better.
  • You are a small investor with a few lakh rupees. The paperwork of LRS, W-8BEN, Form 67 and Schedule FA is the same whatever the size. A fund route can mean fewer forms to track.

Checklist Before You Remit

  • How much of this year's USD 250,000 have education, travel and other remittances already used?
  • Will this year's total remittances cross ₹10 lakh, and can you fund the TCS until you claim it back?
  • Do I know the US-situs value of everything I will hold in a US account, and who inherits it?
  • Have I set up Form 67 and a Schedule FA record for dividends and holdings?
  • For a fund, what is the total expense ratio, and what does the fund's tax note say about gains and dividends?

Frequently Asked Questions

Is GIFT City better than buying US stocks directly?

Neither is better in general. Both use your LRS limit of USD 250,000 a year, both attract 20% TCS above ₹10 lakh of remittances and both need Schedule FA disclosure. Direct investing gives you full choice and exposes you to US estate tax above USD 60,000 of US-situs assets. GIFT City funds give you fewer choices, a fund fee and tax treatment that depends on the structure.

Do I pay tax twice on US dividends?

Not in full. The US withholds 25% under the India-US treaty and India taxes the gross dividend at your slab rate, but you can claim a foreign tax credit by filing Form 67 before your return and reporting it in Schedule TR. The credit is capped at the Indian tax on that income, and excess US tax generally is not refunded.

What is the US estate tax limit for Indians?

The IRS requires the executor of a non-resident's estate to file Form 706-NA when US-situated assets are worth more than USD 60,000 at death, and stock in US corporations counts as US-situated even if held through a nominee. India and the US have no estate tax treaty, and rates can reach 40%. Ask a cross-border tax adviser how it applies to you.

Is the 20% TCS on US investments a final tax?

No. TCS is collected by your bank on remittances above ₹10 lakh in a financial year, for purposes other than education and medical, and you can claim it as credit against your tax liability when you file your return. It still has to be funded up front, so plan your cash flow.

People Also Ask

Can I use GIFT City to avoid LRS?
Not as a resident. Investing in GIFT City funds counts as availing an IFSC financial product, so the amount counts towards the same USD 250,000 annual LRS limit. Once the money is in a GIFT City foreign-currency account, further transactions inside GIFT City do not need a fresh remittance, according to one explainer, but the original remittance does.
Why can't I buy more international mutual funds in India?
Because the industry hit the USD 7 billion overseas investment cap in January 2022. Fund houses can only invest abroad up to the headroom they held in early February 2022, so most international funds stopped taking fresh money. Value Research counted 12 of 66 still taking SIPs on 2 July 2026.
How long must I hold US shares for long-term gains?
More than 24 months. Gains on shares held longer are taxed at 12.5% with no indexation, and gains on shares held 24 months or less are taxed at your slab rate. These are the rates as described by Motilal Oswal in August 2026, so confirm them against the current Finance Act.
