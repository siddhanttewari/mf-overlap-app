SIF Performance, October 2026: Every Live SIF Compared, and How They Stack Up Against Mutual Funds

Twenty-five live SIFs, one table, and the honest picture a year in: a few big winners, a cluster near zero, a few laggards, and four new launches.

📊 Direct Answer

On third-party tracker data to 15 October 2026 (Regular plans), 25 of the 34 live SIFs have return figures. 21 are above their launch price, but only 10 gained over the last three months and 4 over the last month. Three Quant schemes lead with about +29%, +29% and +11% since launch; without Quant's five schemes the middle scheme is up about +2.4% since launch. None is older than about 12 months, and since-launch figures are not yearly, so this is a snapshot, not a ranking to act on.

† A note on Quant's three outliers

Three Quant schemes sit far ahead of everyone else: qSIF Hybrid Long-Short (+29.2% since launch, +21.2% in three months), qSIF Active Asset Allocator (+28.8%, +18.3%) and, less extremely, qSIF Equity Ex-Top 100 (+11.4%). INDmoney reports that a Quant factsheet for July showed average long exposure of about 41%, short about 8% and net about 33% (it does not say for which scheme), and that this does not explain how much of the gain came from each side. Read the monthly portfolio disclosure and the scheme document before reading anything into the number. A strong three months is one market window, not a method.

How SIFs compare with mutual funds

A fair answer has two halves, and they point in slightly different directions.

Staying ahead of an index in a falling stretch is what a hedged fund is built to do. The same funds are not expected to keep up in a strong rally. Our global long-short record shows that trade-off over decades.

What this shows: the SIFs ran a middle level of ups and downs (0.21 to 0.23), more than arbitrage funds (0.07) but less than balanced advantage funds (0.46 to 0.61). Arbitrage funds returned about 6.8% in FY26 with almost no wobble, a useful yardstick for any hybrid SIF given its ₹10 lakh minimum and less frequent redemption. Both SIFs started in October 2025, so their FY26 returns cover only about half a year.

The honest bottom line

After a year there is no proof that SIFs beat mutual funds, and no proof that they do not. What the data does show is that results differ enormously between SIFs that share a label, so the choice of fund matters more than the choice of category. Nothing here is a recommendation to buy or sell any scheme.

Direct or Regular: check which one you are reading

Direct and Regular plans hold the same portfolio. The difference is the yearly cost, because Regular plans pay a distributor. That cost slowly lowers the Regular return, so a quote without the plan name can mislead. The table above is Regular Growth throughout. SIFPrime's July table was for Direct plans, so figures from the two are not directly comparable. Compare Direct with Direct and Regular with Regular.

Criteria before you invest

A good return in the table above is not a reason to start. These are the checks to make first.

Who can invest

The minimum is ₹10 lakh per PAN, counted across all of one fund house's SIF strategies, not per scheme. SEBI-defined accredited investors are exempt. Neither the AMC nor a distributor can lower it.

Your checklist

  • Is ₹10 lakh a small slice? Many advisers keep one non-core bet to roughly 10 to 15% of what you can invest. Our ₹10 lakh framework shows the maths.
  • Can I explain what it does in one sentence? If the long and short parts are a mystery, read our plain-English guide first.
  • What was its net equity exposure? Ask for average long, short and net exposure (not just the label). A Quant factsheet for July, as INDmoney reported it, showed about 41% long, 8% short and 33% net.
  • How long has it existed? Every SIF is under a year old. Treat any return as a first impression.
  • What does it cost? Check the expense ratio for your plan (Direct or Regular) and the exit load in the scheme document. Examples reported in August: 0.50% if sold within 15 days for Magnum and within 30 days for Altiva.
  • When can I get out? Daily, twice a week, weekly? Hybrid and debt SIFs cannot offer daily redemption, and a notice period of up to 15 working days is possible.
  • How will it be taxed? It depends on what the fund holds, not its label. Read our tax guide.
  • Does it overlap with what I already own? Run your funds through the free analyzer first. See also SIF overlap with mutual funds.

What the March 2026 fall showed

SIFPrime calls the March 2026 fall, when the Nifty dropped 11.30%, the category's first stress test. It reports that the gap between the best and worst Hybrid Long-Short fund over that period was more than 8 percentage points, even though they share a label. That gap is the useful lesson: a hedged label does not guarantee a hedged result. Ask any fund how it behaved in that month, not only what it has returned since launch.

How we built this, and what is missing

We took returns from the MeraSIP tracker (the only one we found that shows NAV-based returns for 25 schemes together and says how it calculates them), then compared them with a second tracker dated 16 Oct. The two agree to within 1 percentage point for 22 of 25 schemes since launch and differ by up to about 2 points for a few (some fund houses publish NAVs with a delay, and one tracker's since-launch figure for one scheme did not match its own NAV). So read the decimals as approximate. Neither tracker is an official source: the official daily NAVs are on AMFI.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which SIF has the highest returns in October 2026?

On third-party tracker data to 15 October 2026, Quant's qSIF Hybrid Long-Short Fund leads with about +29.2% since launch (Regular plan), followed by qSIF Active Asset Allocator at about +28.8% and qSIF Equity Ex-Top 100 at about +11.4%. No other tracked scheme is above +9.5%. All are under about 12 months old, so this ranking can change quickly.

Which SIF has performed worst?

ITI's Diviniti Equity Long-Short Fund shows about −12.8% since launch, followed by Franklin Templeton's Sapphire Equity Long-Short at about −4.2% and Union's Arthaya Equity Long-Short at about −3.0% (tracker data to 15 October 2026). Short histories mean these figures can move fast in either direction.

How many SIFs are there in India now?

AMFI reported 34 SIF schemes with assets across 17 fund houses at 30 September 2026. Four more offers have opened since: Tata Titanium Active Asset Allocator and Mahindra Manulife MSIF Equity Long-Short (both closed), and Invesco Summit Active Asset Allocator and AlphaGrep Eminence Hybrid Long-Short (open until 26 and 28 October). SIFPrime counts 38 strategies across 19 fund houses including these.

Do SIFs beat mutual funds?

It is too early to say. Over six months to about September 2026, Cafemutual reported four SIFs ahead of their long-only benchmarks. In August, two hybrid long-short SIFs showed more ups and downs than arbitrage funds but less than balanced advantage funds. No SIF has a full year of history, and results differ widely between SIFs in the same category.

What is the minimum investment in a SIF?

₹10 lakh per PAN, aggregated across all SIF strategies of one fund house. SEBI-defined accredited investors are exempt. Distributors and fund houses cannot reduce it.

Are these Direct or Regular plan returns?

The table on this page uses Regular Growth plans. Direct plans hold the same portfolio at a lower cost, so Direct returns are slightly higher. Always compare like with like.

People Also Ask

Is a high since-launch return a good reason to pick a SIF?
No. Since-launch returns depend on the launch date and are not yearly figures. A fund started in October 2025 has seen a different market from one started in June 2026. Three-month returns over the same window compare better, and even those are short.
Why are some SIF returns so different from others with the same name?
The category label (for example Hybrid Long-Short) only sets broad limits, such as at least 25% in equity and 25% in debt. Each manager decides how much to hold long, how much to short and how much to hedge, so results can differ sharply.
Where can I check official SIF NAVs and returns?
AMFI publishes the official daily NAVs for all SIF schemes, and each fund house shows its own factsheet and portfolio. Trackers such as MeraSIP, SIFPrime and SifScan calculate returns from those NAVs but may lag by a day or differ slightly.
