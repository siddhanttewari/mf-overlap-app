Vested vs INDmoney vs Interactive Brokers: Which App for US Stocks from India?

Three apps, three price lists, one set of tax rules. Here is what each one charges, what you can buy, and which kind of investor each one suits, from their own pages.

📊 Direct Answer

Published brokerage in Oct 2026: Interactive Brokers about USD 1 per small trade, Vested 0.15% (Premium) or 0.25% (Basic, max USD 35), INDmoney 0.25% (max USD 25). But the currency conversion by your bank (INDmoney quotes 0.5% to 1.2%) can cost more than any brokerage, and the taxes are identical on every app: LRS limit USD 250,000, TCS 20% above ₹10 lakh, 12.5% long-term gains, Schedule FA reporting.

The quick answer

All three let a resident Indian buy US stocks and ETFs. They differ in what you pay, what you can buy and how much setup you want. The taxes are the same on every one of them, because they come from your bank and the law, not the app.

🌱

Vested

Fractional shares from USD 1, a free and a paid plan, global funds, curated baskets and a GIFT City arm.

📱

INDmoney

One app for Indian and US investments. Zero platform fees stated, brokerage 0.25% up to USD 25, UCITS ETFs available.

🛠️

Interactive Brokers

A low published commission and access to many overseas markets, with a longer application and a more detailed platform.

Which one fits you?

Vested may fit if…

  • You are new and want a guided setup and fractional shares from USD 1.
  • You want global funds (350+ UCITS funds from USD 50) or ready-made portfolios.
  • You may later want GIFT City, since Vested describes itself as an authorised access provider there.

INDmoney may fit if…

  • You already track your Indian investments in one app and want US stocks alongside.
  • You want free tax and transaction reports and no platform fee.
  • You want London-listed UCITS ETFs with small starting amounts.

Interactive Brokers may fit if…

  • You trade larger amounts or often, where a USD 1 minimum matters.
  • You want bonds or many markets in one account.
  • You are comfortable with a longer application (documents and a webcam check) and a more complex platform.

Your first purchase in 7 steps

  1. Decide what to buy first. Pick the basket, not the app. Our 3-step fund guide helps.
  2. Choose an app and complete KYC. You will need your PAN and address proof. IBKR also asks for income proof and a webcam check.
  3. Ask your bank for the dollar rate. Compare it with the market rate that day. Note whether it charges a wire fee.
  4. Send money under LRS. Your bank will take a declaration. TCS is nil up to ₹10 lakh in the year and 20% above, and you can claim it back.
  5. Buy with a limit order. A limit order sets the highest price you will pay, which protects you from sudden moves.
  6. Keep records. Save statements and dividend details for Form 67 and Schedule FA.
  7. Review once a year. Check overlap with your Indian funds and your total foreign holding.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is the cheapest app to buy US stocks from India?

On published brokerage, Interactive Brokers is lowest per trade (USD 0.005 a share with a USD 1 minimum), Vested Premium is next (0.15%), and Vested Basic and INDmoney are 0.25%. But the currency conversion by your bank can be larger than any of these, so compare the total cost, not just brokerage.

Do the platforms charge extra for currency conversion?

It varies. INDmoney says the 0.5% to 1.2% conversion cost is charged by the bank, not INDmoney. IBKR says it adds no mark-up to quotes but charges a small commission per conversion (minimum USD 2 at smaller volumes). Vested's pricing page does not state a conversion mark-up, so ask before you send money.

Is my money safe on these platforms?

Vested says its broker, VF Securities, Inc., is a FINRA member and SIPC member, with SIPC protection of up to USD 500,000 including USD 250,000 for cash. SIPC protects against a broker failing, not against market losses. Check the same facts for any platform you use.

Can I use more than one app?

Yes. Your LRS limit of USD 250,000 per financial year applies to you across all apps and remittances combined. Keep records for each, because you report all foreign assets in Schedule FA.

Do I pay tax twice on US dividends?

US-listed funds withhold 25% at source for Indian investors under the India-US treaty. You declare the dividend in India and claim credit for the US tax using Form 67, so the credit reduces what you owe. Confirm with a chartered accountant.

Is this different for NRIs?

Yes. Residents use LRS. NRIs have different rules for sending and holding money. See our guide to GIFT City for NRIs vs residents, and check your own residency tax position.

People Also Ask

Is Vested legit?
Vested says its brokerage entity is a FINRA member broker and SIPC member in the US and that it is an authorised access provider at GIFT City regulated by IFSCA. Always verify a platform's registrations on the regulator's own website.
Is INDmoney free for US stocks?
INDmoney lists zero account opening, annual maintenance, platform and withdrawal fees, with brokerage of 0.25% per trade (max USD 25, plus GST). Currency conversion is charged by the bank.
Can Indian residents use Interactive Brokers?
Yes. Interactive Brokers has an India site with account types for residents. On non-Indian markets, residents can trade stocks, bonds and ETFs but not futures, options or margin products.
