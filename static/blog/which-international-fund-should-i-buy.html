Which International Fund Should You Buy? A Simple 3-Step Guide

There is no single best international fund. There is a best fit for what you want to own, the door that is open to you today, and three checks that can save you real money.

📊 Direct Answer

Choose in three steps. (1) Pick the basket you want: the whole world, US companies, emerging markets or one region. (2) Pick an open door: most Indian international mutual funds are closed to new money in Oct 2026, so the real choices are an ETF on NSE (check the premium), a direct route through an LRS app, or GIFT City. (3) Check the premium over NAV, the overlap with what you already own, and the full cost and tax. Do not buy a fund just because it is open or its price is rising.

What is inside a "whole world" fund?

Most people are surprised. A world fund is not split evenly across countries. It is split by company size, and American companies are the biggest. Here is the Vanguard FTSE All-World index (3,821 stocks) as of 30 September 2026:

  • United States62.2%
  • Japan6.1%
  • Taiwan3.5%
  • United Kingdom3.1%
  • Canada2.9%
  • China2.7%
  • South Korea2.6%
  • All other countries16.9%

That fund (a London-listed example, not an NSE fund, and not a recommendation) costs 0.14% a year. The lesson: if you buy a world fund and an S&P 500 fund, you are mostly buying America twice. Quick way to choose

If you want one simple holding and do not want to make country calls, a world fund is the usual starting point. If you want to take a view on America or tech, an S&P 500 or Nasdaq-100 fund is that bet, and it is bigger than it looks. If you already hold Indian funds, remember an emerging markets fund repeats some Indian stocks.

Step 3: Three checks before you click buy

Check 1: Is the price fair? (the premium check)

An ETF has two numbers. The NAV is what the stocks inside are worth. The price is what people pay on the exchange. If price is higher, the difference is called a premium, and it is money that buys nothing.

🧒 Like you are 5

A lunchbox has ₹100 of food inside. If only ten lunchboxes exist and 1,000 kids want one, kids may pay ₹300. The extra ₹200 is the premium. When kids lose interest, the price drops back to ₹100.

The formula is simple: (price − NAV) ÷ NAV. For the Motilal Oswal NASDAQ 100 ETF in the table below, (327.37 − 285.15) ÷ 285.15 = 14.8%. So about ₹15 of every ₹115 you paid was not the shares.

Remember the funds that hold these ETFs

A fund of funds that holds a premium-priced ETF values it at market price, so its returns can jump with the premium. Value Research cited a Mirae S&P 500 Top 50 FoF up 25.6% in a week while its ETF's NAV moved 0.4%.

Three simple ways to put it together

These are illustrations of how people think about it, not advice on what to buy or how much.

1️⃣

The one-fund approach

One world fund. Simple, automatic, no overlap to manage. You accept that about two-thirds is America.

🧩

Core plus a slice

A world fund as the core, plus one small, different slice such as emerging markets or Europe. Check the overlap, because TSMC and India show up in both.

🔧

Fix what I already own

Already hold Nasdaq, S&P and a global fund? Run the overlap check first. Often the answer is to hold fewer funds, not more.

Five mistakes to avoid

  1. Buying because a fund is open. Open does not mean good, and it may close again.
  2. Stacking Nasdaq, S&P and FANG+. More funds, same few companies.
  3. Paying a huge premium. Always compare price with NAV on the day.
  4. Forgetting the paperwork. TCS, Form 67 and Schedule FA are easier if you keep records from day one.
  5. Chasing last year's winner. Emerging markets rose 29.22% in one year to 30 Sep 2026 but only 9.09% a year over ten, against 12.81% for MSCI World (US dollar returns). Past results do not predict the future.

Your checklist before you invest

  • I know which basket I want (world, US, emerging, region or theme).
  • I checked which door is open to me today, not last month.
  • For an ETF: I compared the live price with the NAV and the gap is small.
  • I ran the overlap check against everything I already own.
  • I know my total cost: fees, currency conversion and TCS.
  • I know how the gains and dividends are taxed, and where I report them.
  • I have written down how much I am investing and why.
  • I will re-check this once a year.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is the best international fund for Indian investors?

There is no single best fund. The right choice depends on the basket you want (world, US, emerging markets or a region), the door that is open to you today, and the price, overlap and tax checks. This guide walks through all three steps.

Should I buy a world fund or an S&P 500 fund?

A world fund spreads across thousands of companies and many countries, but it is still about 62 to 64% US. An S&P 500 fund is a bigger bet on one country. If you want one simple holding without country calls, many investors start with a world fund. If you hold both, you are largely buying the same US companies twice.

Are Indian international mutual funds open for investment?

Mostly not. A shared USD 7 billion limit for Indian fund houses is full, so funds can take new money only when room opens. On 25 September 2026 Value Research listed four funds open or reopening, and none was US-focused. Always check the fund house website for today's status.

Is an ETF premium really a problem?

Yes. A premium means you pay more than the stocks inside are worth. In September 2026 the Motilal Oswal Nasdaq Q50 ETF reached a 235.5% premium on 18 September. Compare the price with the NAV on the day you buy.

Do I pay TCS to buy a foreign ETF?

If you send money abroad under LRS for investment, TCS is nil up to ₹10 lakh in a financial year and 20% above that, according to bank disclosures cited by INDmoney and 1Finance. TCS is a prepayment you can claim in your tax return. Confirm with your bank.

Should I buy a US-listed ETF or an Irish-domiciled UCITS ETF?

They can hold the same companies but are taxed differently. US-listed ETF holdings are generally treated as US property for US estate tax, which can create a filing duty above USD 60,000. Irish-domiciled UCITS ETFs are generally treated as non-US property. Talk to a chartered accountant before choosing.

People Also Ask

How much of my money should go into international funds?
There is no single right number. It depends on your goals, time horizon, taxes and how much currency and single-country risk you can accept. Treat any percentage in an article as a starting point for a conversation with a registered adviser.
Can I do SIPs in international funds?
Only in funds that are open to new SIPs. Funds that are shut also stop new SIPs. Some apps let you set up recurring purchases of foreign ETFs, subject to LRS and TCS rules.
What is the difference between a fund of funds and an ETF?
An ETF is bought on the exchange at a market price. A fund of funds is bought from the fund house at NAV and holds another fund or ETF inside. Both can be affected by the underlying ETF's premium.
