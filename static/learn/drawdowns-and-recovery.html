Drawdowns: the fall you have to survive

Average returns look friendly. The real test of an investor is the fall from a peak, and whether they are still holding on at the bottom.

THE 5-YEAR-OLD VERSION

Imagine you fall into a hole. A 10 cm hole is easy to climb out of. A 60 cm hole needs a much longer ladder, and the climb back takes far more effort than the fall did. A drawdown is how deep the hole is, measured from the highest point you had reached.

The cruel arithmetic of recovery

A fall and the gain needed to undo it are not the same number. Lose half your money and you need to double what is left just to get back.

Why this decides your behaviour

In October 2008, an investor looking at a -60% loss had every reason to feel the market was finished. Those who sold near the bottom turned a temporary loss into a permanent one, and missed +76% in 2009. Those who could not sleep at a 60% fall probably should never have held that much in shares in the first place.

A useful question to ask before you invest

Not "how much can I make?" but "what fall can I sit through without selling?" If a 40% drop on paper would make you panic, hold less in shares. That is the point of mixing in gold or debt and sizing positions sensibly. The replay lab lets you rehearse this without risking a rupee.

Remember

  • A drawdown is the fall from the previous peak to the lowest point afterwards.
  • The Nifty 50 fell -60% in 2008 and -38% in 2020. The first took 2.8 years to recover, the second 10 months.
  • A 50% fall needs a 100% gain to recover.
  • Decide in advance how deep a fall you can sit through.
