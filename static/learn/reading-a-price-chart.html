How to read a price chart

A chart is a picture of what already happened. Learning to read it calmly is the first skill of any trader or investor.

THE 5-YEAR-OLD VERSION

A chart is a diary of prices. The left side is the past, the right side is today, and the height is the price. It tells you what happened. It cannot tell you what will happen next.

Two common chart types

  • Line chart: joins each day's closing price. Clean and good for the big picture, like the charts in the previous lessons.
  • Candlestick chart: one "candle" per time period (a day, an hour) showing four prices: open, high, low, close. It shows how the day moved, not just where it ended. Next lesson covers how to read one.

Time frame changes the story

The same market looks calm on a 10-year chart and terrifying on a 10-day chart. A long-term investor and a day trader look at the same prices and see different pictures. Always know which time frame you are looking at, and match it to your own plan.

Linear versus log scale

On a linear scale, the gap between 5,000 and 10,000 looks the same size as the gap between 20,000 and 25,000. But one is a 100% move and the other a 25% move. On a log scale equal percentage moves take equal space, so it is better for long histories. The first chart in this course used a log scale for that reason.

What a chart can and cannot tell you

  • It can show you the trend, how bumpy it has been, and where prices turned before.
  • It cannot tell you why the price moved or what happens next. Treat patterns as descriptions, not promises.

Remember

  • Line charts show the big picture, candles show how each period moved.
  • Time frame changes how risky the same market looks.
  • Log scales show equal percentage moves as equal distances.
  • A chart shows the past. It does not predict.
