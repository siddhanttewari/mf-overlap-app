Candlesticks: a whole day in one picture

Each candle packs a day's fight between buyers and sellers into a single shape.

THE 5-YEAR-OLD VERSION

Imagine a tug of war that lasts all day. A candle records where the rope started (open), the furthest each team pulled it (high and low), and where it stopped when the bell rang (close).

Read two of them:

  • 23 March: opened 7,945.70, fell to a low of 7,583.60 and closed at 7,610.25, down 13.0% on the day. A tall red body: sellers controlled the whole day.
  • 24 March: opened 7,848.30, dipped to 7,511.10, then recovered to close at 7,801.05. A small body with a long lower wick: price was pushed far down but buyers pushed it back up before the close.

Volume: how many traded

Volume is how many shares changed hands. A big move on high volume means many people agreed on the new price; a big move on thin volume can be less convincing. (Index levels have no volume of their own, but individual shares and ETFs do.)

Patterns are names, not predictions

Traders give shapes names ("hammer", "doji", "engulfing"). The names help you describe what happened that day. Treat them as descriptions of past behaviour. Before relying on any pattern to make decisions, test it on lots of data and count the times it failed, which is what the replay lab and the next lesson start to teach.

Remember

  • A candle shows open, high, low and close.
  • The body is open to close, the wicks reach the high and low.
  • A long wick means price travelled far in that direction but came back.
  • Volume shows how many traded. Pattern names describe the past, they do not predict the future.
