Risk, return and the bumpy ride

Higher expected return comes with a bumpier ride. Here is what "bumpy" looked like in real numbers.

THE 5-YEAR-OLD VERSION

Risk is not only "will I lose money". It is "how wide is the range of things that could happen". A calm pond and a wavy sea might both get you across, but in the sea you must be able to stay in the boat when the waves come.

One year at a time

Here is every calendar year of the Nifty 50's price return since 2008:

Notice there is no "typical" year. The average hides a spread from minus 52% to plus 76%. That spread is what we call volatility: how much and how fast the price moves.

Most days are quiet, a few are wild

  • On 71% of trading days the Nifty 50 moved less than 1% either way.
  • The index fell on 47% of days. Even in rising markets almost half the days are down days.
  • Only 43 days in 18 years moved more than 5%, and most of them came in two bursts: 2008 and 2020.
  • The worst day was -13.0% on 23 Mar 2020. The best was +17.7% on 18 May 2009.

Big moves cluster together. Calm periods are long, and then a storm arrives quickly.

Time narrows the range

The same data, but looking at how an investor who held for a longer stretch would have fared (every possible start date, annualised price returns):

Holding periodWorst outcomeBest outcomeShare of start dates that lost money
1 year-56%+99%20%
5 years-2.3% a year+24.8% a year1.4%
10 years+3.8% a year+15.8% a year0%

Be careful with this table

It covers only one market and 18 years, and it starts just before the 2008 peak. Narrowing with time is a pattern in this data, not a guarantee. A longer horizon makes losses less likely, never impossible.

Remember

  • Risk is the width of the range of outcomes, not just the chance of a loss.
  • Yearly returns in this data ran from minus 52% to plus 76%.
  • Most days are small; a handful of huge days arrive in bursts.
  • Longer holding periods narrowed the range in this data, but there is no guarantee.
