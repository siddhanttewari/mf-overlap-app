Stocks, indexes and funds: what is the difference?

You hear "Nifty", "index fund" and "ETF" as if they were the same thing. They are not, and the difference decides what you actually own.

THE 5-YEAR-OLD VERSION

A stock is one apple. An index is a list of the 50 biggest apples in the orchard, with a scoreboard that shows how they are doing together. You cannot buy the scoreboard. A fund is a basket that someone fills with apples for you. An index fund is a basket that copies the list exactly.

The four things, side by side

ThingWhat it isCan you buy it?
StockOne company, for example InfosysYes, through a broker
IndexA list and a scoreboard, for example Nifty 50 (50 large companies picked by NSE Indices)No, it is only a measure
Mutual fundA pool of many investors' money, run by a fund house (AMC), holding many stocksYes, from the AMC or an app
ETFA fund that trades on the exchange like a share, often copying an indexYes, through a broker at a live price

An index fund is a mutual fund that simply copies an index, so it holds the same companies in the same proportions. An active fund has a manager who picks stocks, trying to beat the index, and charges more for it.

What is inside the Nifty 50?

The Nifty 50 is weighted by company size (free-float market capitalisation), so bigger companies count for more. These ten make up the largest part:

HDFC Bank10.38%
ICICI Bank9.05%
Reliance Industries7.58%
Bharti Airtel5.10%
Larsen & Toubro4.20%
State Bank of India3.79%
Axis Bank3.37%
Infosys3.35%
Kotak Mahindra Bank2.93%
Mahindra & Mahindra2.52%

Weights as published by NSE Indices on 30 Sep 2026 (read 17 Oct 2026). The ten together are 52.3% of the index.

So an index fund on the Nifty 50 puts about 52 paise of every rupee into just ten companies, with banks well represented. "50 companies" does not mean 50 equal bets.

Why this matters when you own more than one fund

If you own a Nifty 50 index fund, a large-cap fund and a flexi-cap fund, you probably own HDFC Bank and Reliance three times. That is called overlap. Remember

  • An index is a measuring scoreboard, not something you buy. Index funds and ETFs let you own what it measures.
  • ETFs trade on the exchange all day, mutual funds are bought from the fund house at the day's price (NAV).
  • The top 10 Nifty 50 companies are 52% of the index, so it is more concentrated than "50 companies" sounds.
  • Several funds can hold the same companies, which is overlap.
